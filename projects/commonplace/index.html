What you're actually looking at

The ink above is not a video and not an image. It's a WebGL fluid simulation reading the headline as a live texture — which is why the letterforms bleed and swim instead of sitting politely under a coloured overlay. The words are still real text. Select them. Tab into the field and type. The button still takes your clicks.

That comes from Canvas UI riding the html-in-canvas proposal: drawElementImage() hands a canvas a rasterised copy of live DOM every frame, while the DOM underneath stays laid out, focusable and clickable. It's a proposal, not a standard, and today it needs chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features in a Chromium browser.

Without that flag — which is to say, for essentially every real visitor right now — the shader has no page texture to bend, so it washes ink over the type instead of through it. The two states are tuned separately here, because a fluid sim at full strength does not politely flow around your paragraphs. It covers them.

Fluid component by David Haz (MIT + Commons Clause), recoloured and retuned. The simulation itself is unmodified.
